In a study with 77 U.S. participants, fictional company webpages with AI-generated hero images were rated at least as high as those with real stock photos on trust, professionalism, and authenticity when participants did not know the images were AI-generated. The only statistically significant difference was a small 0.4-point increase in authenticity ratings for AI images on a 1–7 scale.

This finding suggests that design teams can consider AI-generated images as a viable alternative to stock photos without sacrificing user trust or perceived professionalism, as long as the AI origin is not disclosed. However, the study only tested 6 workplace images on a fictional consulting website, so results may not generalize to other industries or image types.

Participants' perceptions were influenced by cultural and gender representation in images, with diversity and inclusion mentioned frequently in open-ended feedback. This highlights the importance of representative imagery, whether AI-generated or stock.

The study did not test scenarios where images are clearly labeled as AI-generated, so disclosure effects remain unknown. Additionally, the limited image set may not represent all contexts.
