New EU AI labelling requirements, based on Article 50(4) of the EU AI Act, took effect on 2 August 2026 and apply to any company serving EU citizens, including non-EU companies. The obligations cover deepfakes, chatbots and AI agents, fully AI-written text on matters of public interest without human review, and emotion recognition and biometric categorization tools.

For designers and product teams, the EU Commission has published an official EU AI icon set for labelling, but using an icon alone does not establish legal compliance. Labels must be clearly visible, in plain language, accessible to assistive technologies, and persist when content is reshared or downloaded. Exceptions apply when AI-generated text has been reviewed and edited by a human with a named person or entity taking editorial responsibility, and small assistive edits like spellcheck, grammar, cropping, and translation do not count as AI generation.

The rule stems from Article 50(4) of the EU AI Act, which mandates labelling of AI-generated content. This affects any company offering services to EU citizens, regardless of where the company is based. For fully AI-written text on public-interest matters without human review, labelling is required to avoid misleading the public.
