The PROVE framework evaluates one AI tool against one specific task using five dimensions: Problem Alignment, Risk, Output Quality, Velocity, and Experience. In the author's Gemini Notebooks evaluation, that led to a one-month trial rather than immediate permanent adoption.

The framework is scoped to individual tool-versus-task decisions; it is not meant for organization-wide tool recommendations or for characterizing monetary costs of prolonged use. For practitioners making adoption calls, this keeps the decision tied to the actual workflow being considered.

The evaluation grew out of a recurring editorial task: writing a digest from the same three sources. The author measured hand-writing the digest at about 25 minutes and the Gemini Notebooks version at a little over 10 minutes, including voice edits. The AI output was accurate and complete but read like a report rather than a person, so it still needed voice editing before posting, and the AI workflow added two extra handoffs per run compared with the manual three-step process.

The reported time savings and ratings come from a single self-evaluation by the author on one recurring task, so they may not generalize to other tools or workflows; the article notes that one run is a screen, not a verdict.
