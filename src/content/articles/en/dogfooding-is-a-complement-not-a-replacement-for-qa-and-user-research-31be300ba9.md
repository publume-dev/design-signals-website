When employees use their own product before release, their struggles are a strong signal: if they have trouble, users will likely struggle even more. But employee success does not guarantee user success, according to Nielsen Norman Group.

That is why dogfooding cannot replace user research: employees suffer from the curse of knowledge and cannot represent real users' mental models. Teams still need QA testing by professionals to assess reliability through structured testing, and user research to observe whether representative users can accomplish their goals.

The limits are especially visible with AI systems, where reliability is harder to assess because outputs are probabilistic. Dogfooding adds an extra perspective but remains a complement, not a replacement for QA and user research.
